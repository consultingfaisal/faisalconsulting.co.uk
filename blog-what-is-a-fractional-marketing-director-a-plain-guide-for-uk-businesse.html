What is a fractional marketing director? A plain guide for UK businesses

15 November 2026

Fractional marketing director is a title many UK business owners meet for the first time when they start looking for senior marketing help. It sounds like jargon. The idea behind it is simple.

A fractional marketing director is a senior marketing leader who works for your business for part of the week, on a regular basis, and owns the results of your marketing in that time. You get the seniority of a marketing director without employing one full time.

This guide explains what the role covers, how the time usually works, what it is not, and what your business needs to have ready. It does not cover fees. Those depend on scope, and there is a separate page on this site about what drives them.

The short definition

Fractional means a share. You buy a fixed share of one senior person's time, such as a set number of days a week or a month. The rest of their week goes to other clients.

Marketing director means the most senior marketing seat in the business. The person in that seat sets the marketing plan, decides where the budget goes, leads the people who do the work and answers for what marketing delivers.

Put the two together and you have someone who does the director's job, not a junior version of it, but on part of the week. You may also see the phrase fractional marketing used for this kind of arrangement. It usually means the same thing: senior marketing leadership bought by the share of time rather than as a full time salary.

What the role usually owns

The scope is agreed at the start, so it varies. Most engagements cover the same core areas, because these are the parts of marketing that need a senior owner.

The common thread is ownership. A fractional marketing director is there to be accountable for an outcome, not to produce a report and leave.

  • The marketing plan: agreeing what marketing is meant to achieve for the business, then setting the priorities to get there.
  • The budget: deciding where money goes, and moving it when the evidence says something is not working.
  • Measurement: making sure tracking and reporting show what marketing actually produces, not just how busy it is.
  • People and agencies: briefing, leading and holding your team and your agencies to the same goal.
  • Reporting upwards: giving the owner or board one clear view of marketing performance and what happens next.

How the time works in practice

Most arrangements are built around a regular rhythm. That might be one or two fixed days each week, or a set number of days spread across a month. A fixed pattern helps your team know when decisions will be made.

The days themselves usually mix planning, meetings with your team and agencies, and reviewing results. Between those days, a good fractional director stays reachable for decisions that cannot wait, within limits you agree up front.

Some businesses start with more time in the first weeks, while the director gets to know the numbers and the people, and then settle into a steadier pattern. Others keep the same level throughout. Agree the pattern before you start, and agree how you will review it.

  • Ask how many days a week or month you are buying, and whether they are fixed days.
  • Ask how contact works between those days, and what counts as urgent.
  • Ask how often you will review whether the time is still right.

What a fractional marketing director is not

The title is easy to confuse with other kinds of outside help. The difference matters, because it changes who owns the result.

A marketing agency carries out work it has been briefed to do. It can be very good at that work, but it does not usually own your marketing plan or answer for your commercial results across every channel.

A consultant or adviser reviews, recommends or coaches. That is useful, but the work of putting the advice into practice stays with you.

An interim marketing director works full time for a fixed period, often to cover a gap such as a departure or long leave. A fractional director works part time over a longer run.

A full time marketing director is an employee who gives the business all of their working week. That makes sense when there is enough senior work to fill it. If you are weighing up the two job titles, chief marketing officer and marketing director, there is a separate post on this site that compares them.

Who it tends to suit

A fractional marketing director tends to suit a business that needs senior marketing judgement but does not yet have a full week of senior marketing work. That is often a growing company with a small team, a business with agencies but no one in house to lead them, or an owner who is still making the marketing decisions alongside everything else.

It is less of a fit when what you need is mostly hands on delivery. If the plan is clear and the gap is simply capacity to run campaigns, more delivery help may serve you better than more leadership.

It is also less of a fit when nobody in the business can make time to work with the director. Part time leadership still needs a full time business behind it to act on decisions.

What to have ready before one starts

A fractional director has fewer hours with you than an employee would, so the first weeks matter. You can save a lot of that time by getting a few things ready in advance.

None of this needs to be perfect. The point is that the director spends the first days understanding your business, not chasing logins.

  • Access to your analytics, ad accounts, website and customer data, or a named person who can grant it quickly.
  • Your current agency contracts, briefs and the last few months of reports.
  • A clear sponsor in the business, usually the owner or managing director, who can make decisions.
  • An honest view of what you think is working and what is not, even if you are unsure.
  • Agreement on the commercial result marketing should be judged on, such as qualified leads or sales.

How you will know it is working

Agree at the start what success looks like and when you will check it. A useful engagement should leave you with clearer priorities, numbers you trust, and a team and agencies who know what they are working towards.

Ask for a regular written summary of what changed, what it produced and what comes next. If you cannot tell what the director has done after the first few months, raise it early.

Also agree how the engagement ends. Some businesses grow into a full time marketing hire, and part of the fractional role is helping to make that hire and hand over cleanly. Others keep a fractional arrangement for as long as it fits.

A fractional marketing director is a simple idea: a senior owner for your marketing, on part of the week. The useful questions are what they will own, how the time will work, and what result you will judge them on.
