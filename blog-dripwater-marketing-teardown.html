Drip Water: what their public marketing signals are actually saying

19 September 2026 · Marketing Teardown

Pick up a can of Drip Water in Morrisons, Tesco, or Holland & Barrett and check the small print. The return address printed on it is "www.thedrip.vip" — not dripwater.co.uk, or anything that obviously reads as the brand on the can in your hand.

That's a small thing. Here's a bigger one, sitting right next to it, unused: when Drip's regular can supplier fell short, the company bought 80,000 unused aluminium bottles from a collapsed rival, Almighty Water, checked the move with a Trading Standards officer, and re-sleeved them in its own branding rather than manufacture new cans from scratch.

That's a genuinely rare, specific, hard-to-copy sustainability story — miles better than the generic "plastic bad, cans good" line running on the actual product copy right now. It's reported in a specialist trade publication. It isn't on the can, the website, or anywhere a buying customer would ever see it.

Those two findings, and two more, are laid out in a one-page breakdown below, built entirely from Drip's own product packaging as listed by major retailers, and trade press reporting quoting the company directly. Nothing here claims any insight into revenue, sales volume, or internal reporting.
