Riyadh Season marketing teardown: booking UX and visitor planning

21 September 2026 · Evidence-led outside-in review

Riyadh Season spans many zones, formats and booking rules. webook.com provides ticketing, resale and fan-protection infrastructure. At that scale, the highest-leverage marketing question is whether a visitor can confidently decide what to do today, nearby, with their group.

Scope and limitation: public sources only. This review does not assess revenue, campaign performance, customer data or internal strategy. It separates what was visible on 21 September 2026 from hypotheses that require internal validation.

The wider lesson

For a large event ecosystem, planning confidence is part of the experience being sold.

Observed 01

The official platform covers many tasks

webook.com carries events, experiences, sports, restaurants, resale and subscriptions. It also publishes fan-protection guidance and anti-fraud controls.

Observed 02

Decision detail lives at event level

Individual listings carry dates, suitability, schedules and package details. That depth is useful, but it can require visitors to inspect several pages before comparing options.

Possible implication

A hypothesis, not a verdict

A visitor may not need more campaign reach; they may need a faster way to turn the catalogue into a workable plan. Better cross-event comparison could improve confidence without changing inventory.

Alternative explanation

A credible reason the signal may be benign

The app, personalisation and location services may already solve this for logged-in users. Broad public pages may be designed for discovery, with planning deliberately deferred until intent is clearer.

The smallest useful test

Learn before committing to a larger fix

Prototype a 'Today in Riyadh' view organised by time, distance, availability, audience and booking requirement. Test task completion against the current browse journey with residents and visitors.

What I would check internally

  1. Can a visitor compare several zones without opening each listing?
  2. Are booking requirements and availability visible before the detail page?
  3. How often do users leave to build an itinerary elsewhere?
  4. Which planning questions drive search, support and abandonment?
