Offline conversions: getting phone and WhatsApp leads back into Google Ads

25 November 2026

Plenty of businesses take the real decision off the website. Someone clicks an ad, then rings the office or sends a WhatsApp message. The quote, the booking or the sale happens in that conversation.

Google Ads only knows what it is told. If the account counts a click on a phone number or a form fill, that is what it learns to find more of. It has no idea which of those people became customers and which never answered the follow up call.

Offline conversions close that gap. You record what happened to each lead after the click, then send it back to Google Ads. This guide covers the methods Google offers, what each one needs, and the checks that stop the data going wrong. It is method only. The right set up depends on how your leads arrive and where you keep them.

Why the account needs the outcome, not the enquiry

Automated bidding learns from the conversions you give it. If every enquiry counts the same, a time waster and a good customer look identical. The system will happily buy more of whichever is cheaper.

Feeding back the outcome changes the question the account is answering. Instead of finding people who press call, it starts looking for people who become customers. That only works if the data is complete and arrives on time, which is what the rest of this guide is about.

Before you start, write down which stage you want to count. A qualified lead, a booked appointment and a paid sale are all fair choices. Pick one that happens often enough to give the account something to learn from, and that your team records consistently.

Phone calls: the four ways Google can see them

Google's help pages describe four kinds of phone call conversion. They are not equal, so it is worth knowing which one your account is using.

For calls from ads and calls to your website number, you set a minimum call length, and every call at least that long is counted. Set it to something that reflects a real conversation for your business, not a missed call or a wrong number. Then check it against your phone records for a week or two.

Clicks on a number are the weakest signal of the four. If that is all the account is counting, treat the conversion figure with care.

  • Calls from ads. These come from call assets or call only ads, where someone taps to call straight from the ad.
  • Calls to a number on your website. A Google forwarding number replaces your number on the page, so the call can be tied back to the ad click. This needs the Google tag and a phone snippet, and the domain has to be verified.
  • Clicks on a number on your mobile site. This counts the tap, not the call. Someone can tap and hang up, and it still counts.
  • Imported call conversions. You send Google the caller's phone number and what happened later, such as a sale. This uses Google forwarding numbers too.

Forms and calls you log in a CRM: importing by click ID

When auto tagging is on, Google adds a click identifier, called a GCLID, to the landing page address. Google's own instructions for offline import ask you to do three things: turn on auto tagging, capture the GCLID on your web pages, and store it against the lead's record, for example in your CRM.

In practice that usually means a hidden field on your enquiry form that picks up the GCLID and passes it into the CRM with the lead. When the lead later becomes a sale, you upload the GCLID with the conversion name and the time it happened.

Three rules from Google's guidance matter most.

  • Offline conversions uploaded more than 90 days after the last click are not imported. A long sales cycle needs a plan for this.
  • Google asks you to upload at least daily, or on a steady schedule such as every two days or weekly if daily is not possible.
  • The same GCLID, conversion name and conversion time is only counted once, so a repeat upload does not double your numbers.

Enhanced conversions for leads

Google describes enhanced conversions for leads as an upgraded version of offline conversion import. Instead of relying only on the click ID, it uses information the person gave you, such as an email address or phone number, sent in hashed form. Google matches it to the original lead.

It needs the Google tag or Google Tag Manager on your site to pick up that information when the form is submitted. You then send the outcome later, for example through Google Ads Data Manager. Google still asks you to include the GCLID whenever you have it.

Google's help pages recommend starting with enhanced conversions for leads if you have not set up offline import yet, and recommend that existing offline import users upgrade. Note the shorter window: uploads that include this personal data must be no more than 63 days old.

Because it uses customer data, read Google's customer data policies before you switch it on, and check that your privacy notice and consent set up cover it.

WhatsApp and other messages

Google Ads has message assets, which add a button to search ads that opens a chat. Google's pages list WhatsApp, SMS, Facebook Messenger and Zalo, and at the time of writing describe the feature as in beta. Eligibility depends on policy history, your sector and advertiser verification.

Starting a chat is not the same as becoming a customer. Treat it like a click on a phone number: a sign of interest. To count the outcome, you need a way to link the chat to a lead record. The simplest route is to ask for an email address or phone number in the conversation, log the lead in the CRM, and send the result back through enhanced conversions for leads.

If a chat number is just printed on your website, Google cannot see the chat at all unless you capture the details and import them.

Checks before you let bidding use the data

Bad offline data is worse than none, because bidding will act on it. Run through these checks first.

  • Take ten recent sales from the CRM and confirm each one has a click ID or a contact detail that can be matched.
  • Compare the number of conversions Google shows with your own records for the same weeks. A big gap needs an explanation before anything else.
  • Check who uploads, how often, and what happens when they are on holiday. A missed week is a gap the account cannot recover after 90 days.
  • Make sure the old and new conversions are not both set as primary goals, or the same sale may be counted twice.
  • Note the date you changed the goals, so a jump in reported conversions is not mistaken for better performance.

Getting offline results back into Google Ads is less about the tool and more about the habit: capture the click, record the outcome, send it on time.
