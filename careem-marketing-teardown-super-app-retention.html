Careem marketing teardown: can an everything app explain what to use next?

23 September 2026 · Evidence-led outside-in review

Careem publicly describes itself as the everything app. The English-language journey spans rides, taxis, bikes, food, dining, groceries, payments, international transfers and other everyday services. Careem Plus then connects several categories through one subscription. The outside-in opportunity is helping a customer discover the next genuinely relevant service without turning the app into a catalogue.

Scope and limitation: public sources only. This review does not assess revenue, campaign performance, customer data or internal strategy. It separates what was visible on 23 September 2026 from hypotheses that require internal validation.

The wider lesson

A super app creates more value when the next service feels useful, not merely available.

Observed 01

The proposition is organised around everyday jobs

The public site groups services under actions such as Go, Eat, Get and Pay. That gives the breadth a simple organising idea while still exposing many individual products within each group.

Observed 02

Careem Plus makes cross-service value explicit

The subscription brings together savings across rides, food, groceries, dining and other services. Its value therefore depends not only on frequency, but on whether members use more than one relevant category.

Possible implication

Breadth can improve retention or create discovery friction

A ride customer may have no reason to explore payments at that moment. A customer who repeatedly orders food may benefit from Plus immediately. The growth question is when the next service becomes contextually helpful rather than distracting.

Alternative explanation

A credible reason the signal may be benign

The signed-in app may already personalise service order, offers and prompts using location, history and availability. Public web pages show the portfolio, not the decisioning experienced by individual customers.

The smallest useful test

Learn before committing to a larger fix

After one completed task, test a single next-service message chosen from behaviour and context, with a clear reason for relevance. Compare second-category adoption and repeat use against a generic multi-service promotion, while monitoring dismissals.

What I would check internally

  1. Which first service most often leads to sustainable second-category use?
  2. Can customers estimate Careem Plus value from their actual monthly behaviour?
  3. When does a cross-service prompt help rather than interrupt the primary task?
  4. Are service availability, pricing and benefits consistent across web and app entry points?
