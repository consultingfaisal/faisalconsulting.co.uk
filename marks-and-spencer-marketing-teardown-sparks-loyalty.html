Marks & Spencer marketing teardown: can Sparks connect food, fashion and home?

23 September 2026 · Evidence-led outside-in review

Marks & Spencer has several powerful reasons to visit: food, clothing, beauty, home and gifting. Sparks is the public mechanism intended to connect those visits. The outside-in question is whether customers experience one useful relationship with M&S, or several category journeys sharing the same login.

Scope and limitation: public sources only. This review does not assess revenue, campaign performance, customer data or internal strategy. It separates what was visible on 23 September 2026 from hypotheses that require internal validation.

The wider lesson

A loyalty programme becomes more valuable when it connects customer missions, not only transactions.

Observed 01

Sparks now makes its value more tangible

The public programme has moved towards pounds in a digital wallet, personalised offers, birthday benefits and hot-drink rewards. That is easier to explain than a vague promise of future recognition.

Observed 02

The brand still contains very different shopping missions

Food, fashion, beauty, home and financial products sit under the M&S name, but each has a different purchase frequency and decision process. A single rewards explanation does not automatically make those journeys feel connected.

Possible implication

The opportunity is cross-category usefulness

A customer may understand Sparks and still use it within one category only. The commercial question is whether the programme helps someone discover a relevant second reason to return without making personalisation feel intrusive.

Alternative explanation

A credible reason the signal may be benign

M&S may already deliver strong cross-category personalisation inside the signed-in app, email programme and customer data environment. Public pages cannot show the quality of those individual experiences.

The smallest useful test

Learn before committing to a larger fix

Run a controlled post-purchase test that introduces one genuinely relevant adjacent category benefit. Compare second-category purchase, opt-out and repeat visit rates against a standard reward message.

What I would check internally

  1. What share of active Sparks customers purchase across more than one M&S category?
  2. Which category combinations create repeat value rather than one-off discount use?
  3. Can customers explain the difference between Sparks rewards and M&S financial-product rewards?
  4. Does personalisation increase category discovery without increasing message fatigue?
