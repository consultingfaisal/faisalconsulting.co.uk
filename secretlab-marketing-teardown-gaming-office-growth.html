Secretlab marketing teardown: can gaming authority travel into the office?

23 September 2026 · Evidence-led outside-in review

Secretlab built its authority around gaming chairs and performance setups. Its public range now distinguishes the TITAN Evo as a flagship ergonomic chair and ATLAS as an ergonomic task chair for focused work. That creates a useful growth question: how does the brand recruit office buyers without making its original expertise feel less distinctive?

Scope and limitation: public sources only. This review does not assess revenue, campaign performance, customer data or internal strategy. It separates what was visible on 23 September 2026 from hypotheses that require internal validation.

The wider lesson

Category expansion works best when the new product borrows trust without borrowing the old customer's assumptions.

Observed 01

The range now names two different work modes

Secretlab positions TITAN Evo for a broad range of postures and ATLAS for task-focused work. The distinction is meaningful because the products can appeal to overlapping buyers while solving different expectations.

Observed 02

The direct model has to carry the confidence burden

Public product pages emphasise direct pricing, shipping, trials, materials and engineering. For a premium chair that many customers cannot test locally, comparison and reassurance become part of the product experience.

Possible implication

A new office audience may not use gaming as its proof point

Existing fans already understand the brand. A professional buyer may instead compare ATLAS with established office furniture and ask about prolonged task use, fit, warranty and returns. The same evidence may need a different order.

Alternative explanation

A credible reason the signal may be benign

Secretlab may intentionally keep the range close to its gaming roots because that distinctiveness is commercially valuable. Reviews, creators and regional campaigns may already provide the audience-specific proof not visible on core pages.

The smallest useful test

Learn before committing to a larger fix

Test a role-led comparison for buyers choosing between TITAN Evo and ATLAS. Start with daily tasks, movement and setup rather than product names. Measure comparison completion, product-page progression, returns questions and conversion by declared use case.

What I would check internally

  1. Which search terms bring office-first prospects rather than existing gaming audiences?
  2. Can first-time buyers explain the functional difference between TITAN Evo and ATLAS?
  3. Which proof points reduce hesitation when a customer cannot try the chair in person?
  4. Does office acquisition expand the audience without weakening gaming consideration?
